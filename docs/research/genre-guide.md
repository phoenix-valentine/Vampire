## 1. What makes the best games good, and how games fail

### Must-haves (players expect them)

| Item | Evidence |
|---|---|
| One input: move. The game aims and fires | Luca Galante cites a 2019 phone game, Magic Survival, as his major inspiration; auto-attack is rare outside phones [S3][S17]. Funday debated a dash for its spin-off and rejected it because it pulled attention from the core [S12] |
| A small reward every few seconds, a choice every 30 to 60 seconds | Vampire Survivors: level-up windows "every 30 seconds to 1 minute"; 3 options, a 4th by a Luck stat; reroll and banish [S23]. Brotato is recommended for "making a decision every minute" [S9] |
| A power curve from weak to screen-clearing inside one run | One commentary: the 40-hour RPG power fantasy compressed into twenty minutes (probably [S22]); Jeff Vogel's review title: "Five Bucks To Feel Like a God" [S6] |
| Upgrades that combine into a build | Brotato's stated goal: hordes plus an auto-chess shop for "unique builds through weapon synergies" [S7]. Reviews name synergies and weapon evolutions as central (first search; pages not identified). Evolutions are the founder's signature system: take the idea of combining, not the recipes |
| Loud feedback on kills, pickups and the rare big reward | Galante used his slot-machine graphics experience for the chest-opening moment [S4]; the reward structure is what brings players back [S5]. That fanfare is Poncle's own presentation: take the idea of a rare, celebrated reward, not its look |
| Something kept between runs | A 2026 buyer's guide: unlocks between runs keep players going (probably [S26]); Magic Survival already had it in 2019 [S17] |

### Differentiators (where games compete)

| Item | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Where the choice sits and how often | Brotato: a shop between waves of 20 to 90 seconds, Thomas Gervraud's own mix of two genres [S7][S8]. Halls of Torment: gear and character progression in the style of action RPGs [S11] |
| A second verb that comes from the theme | Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor adds mining, "a layer to the gameplay loop that simply isn't there in other auto-shooters" [S13]. Mining is that game's identity |
| More to do with movement | Megabonk's 3D jumping and sliding answer the complaint that 2D games "feel passive once the build comes together" [S19] |
| Readable endgame | A ResetEra poster prefers Halls of Torment for "readable endgame visuals" [S10] |
| Difficulty that holds, or can be tuned | The same poster: it holds "right to the end"; another found it "a bit too easy" [S10]. Its 1.0 added a shrine to raise difficulty [S11] |
| Co-op | The Spell Brigade (4 players, 1.0 in April 2026) is among the most played [S22] |
| Polish and a look of its own | "High fidelity and polish, or some degree of pivot" is now needed; players notice reskins [S1] |

### Common failures (what reviews complain about)

| Failure | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Late-run clutter hides the player and the threats | "You die faster than them, because you can't see them in your effects" (DRG: Survivor thread); late Vampire Survivors runs "literally can't see anything", with weapon effects drawn above the player; in Soulstone Survivors, at 25% effect visibility enemy attack outlines are "still barely visible" [S14]. Survivor.io: enemies "almost impossible to see" [S16]. An effects slider is called a patch, not a fix [S14] |
| Passive play, no tension | Vogel: "no balancing and no downsides" [S6]; "the most you do is selecting what upgrades to get" [S25] |
| Slow or samey start | DRG: Survivor "a little samey for the first few hours" [S13]; Army of Ruin "too slow", missing the reward loop [S25] |
| Grind felt as a gate | Games "engineered to get me stuck for 200 hours" (2026 critique in the first search; page not identified). Survivor.io: pay-to-win and energy are the top complaints [S16] |
| Luck decides the run | Survivor.io "frustratingly random" [S16]; Megabonk "difficulty spikes" [S19] |
| A second system crowds out the core | Funday spent about a month adding missions, then cut them: "too much about the mission, and not enough about deleting everything on the screen and mining" [S12] |
| Looks like a reskin | "Most clones ... don't bring anything new" (Ghost Ship, quoted in [S1b]); derivative 2D sprite games are singled out [S1] |
| On phones: long sessions, no suspend, slowdown | See section 5 |

## 2. Which of the 16 blocks carry the weight

| Weight | Block | Why in this genre |
|---|---|---|
| Highest | 7 progression and rewards | The level-up choice is the player's main decision and the reward rhythm is the hook [S5][S7][S23] |
| Highest | 6 difficulty and pacing | Enemy pressure against player power is the content. The founder is criticised for no tension [S6]; challengers are praised for holding it [S10] |
| Highest | 3 feedback and feel | The game fires for you, so the pleasure is in how kills, pickups and rare rewards land [S4] |
| Highest | 9 readability and interface | The genre's signature failure [S14] |
| High | 1 core loop | Move, kill, collect, choose must please in the first 20 seconds with one weapon |
| High here | 2 controls | Only one input, so any fault is felt every second |
| High here | 16 platform, 8 session structure | Hundreds of objects on a phone; slowdown reported even on an iPhone 13 Pro [S18]; long runs are a phone complaint [S16] |
| Middle | 4 rules, 5 content, 11 audio, 10 art | Content count drives replay only after the loop works; art serves readability first |
| Low | 12 theme, 13 onboarding, 14 menus, 15 saving | The founder's theme explains nothing (M) and one input teaches itself. Exception: mid-run suspend on phones [S15] |

Warning for the prototype (R): players judge "fun" in this genre largely through blocks 6 and 7. A prototype of blocks 1 to 4 with no level-up choice and no pressure ramp would test a different game. Include a minimal version of both.

## 4. State of the genre, 2025 and 2026

- **Crowded.** "Very saturated"; "no longer a quick-win-rapid-release genre" but still viable for small teams with excellent quality (2026-05-19) [S1]. Every Steam Next Fest was "filled to the brim" (2025-04-20) [S2]. Summer 2026 was called the busiest release calendar the format has had [S22]. Release counts and median revenue: not found.
- **Recognised.** A public poll (8,023 votes, closed 2025-12-11) chose "Bullet Heaven" with 44%, against 31% for "Survivors-like"; Valve added the tag in May 2026 [S20].
- **What worked.** Megabonk (3D, one developer, $9.99): 1 million copies in two weeks, peak 117,336 players, 94% positive [S19]. DRG: Survivor 1.0: 90/100 from PC Gamer, "Vampire Survivors is no longer the king of the genre" [S13]. Vital Shell succeeded as "just a 20% improvement on the core formula" [S1]. Most played in October 2026: Megabonk, DRG: Survivor, The Spell Brigade [S22]. Reddit's top three: Soulstone Survivors, DRG: Survivor, 20 Minutes Till Dawn [S21].
- **Pattern.** Winners keep the formula and change one thing. Funday: keep innovation "as low as possible", "this game but with that" [S12]. Reviewers praised Megabonk for staying faithful where others "add twists that break the formula" [S19].
- **Tired of.** Reskins, passive play, grind, clutter, samey first hours, cheap phone knock-offs [S1][S2][S14][S16][S25].

## 5. Phone and web specifics

- **The genre began on phones.** Magic Survival (2019) is free with an advert after death and a one-time $3.99 removal; rated 9.5/10 on TapTap from 178 ratings [S17].
- **Portrait with one finger works.** A 148Apps reviewer prefers it for Vampire Survivors mobile and plays "almost anywhere" [S15]. Not verified: portrait shows less to the sides, so side threats need earlier warning (R).
- **Session.** Survivor.io levels run 8 to 15 minutes of clock time, up to 30 real minutes because choices and bosses stop the clock; one reviewer calls this tedious [S16]. Vampire Survivors mobile is criticised for no mid-run suspend [S15] (weak source). The owner's 1 to 5 minutes is shorter than every game checked, so the whole arc must be compressed.
- **Performance is a known phone failure.** Vampire Survivors mobile lost "speed and responsiveness" in the port [S15]; 20 Minutes Till Dawn slowed down "even on an iPhone 13 Pro" [S18].
- **Money shapes phone reviews.** Free with optional adverts is praised [S15]; energy and paid power are the top complaints [S16].
- **Thumb.** Implementation notes agree: joystick appears where the thumb lands, small dead zone, clear of the home bar, left-handed option, avatar never under the thumb [S24] (weak sources).
- **Web** (all M, unverified): the founder started as a free browser game; iOS Safari needs a tap before sound, has no vibration API, can clear saved data, and page gestures (scroll, pull-to-refresh, zoom) must be blocked.

## 7. What this means for the prototype (R)

Three variations that match the genre's three proven structures:

1. **Continuous swarm.** One rising timer, a level-up every 15 to 20 s.
2. **Short waves.** 20 s first wave, +5 s each, one choice between waves.
3. **Second verb from the theme.** Feeding as the equivalent of mining, kept subordinate to the killing (Funday's lesson [S12]).

In all three: player drawn above every effect, one reserved colour for what hurts, a cap on effects per weapon, and death inside the first minute must be possible.
