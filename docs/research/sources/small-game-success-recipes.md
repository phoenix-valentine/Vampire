# Small Game Success Recipes (pages 1 to 14)

Source: `724d981c-Small_Game_Success_Recipes.pdf`, dated Nov 20, 2026, by @Phoenix [p. 1]. All 14 pages read; none unreadable. Link targets are not visible in the page images, only the link text. Longer than the 1800-word target because every table is kept whole.

## What it is

A data summary of what the 485 small hit games in the owner's "Remake Ladder" (1970-2026) share, written to the owner ("your itch.io games", "your Game Building Blocks guide") [p. 1, 3, 8]. It gives five recipes, six shared ingredients, ten modern practices, a table of trends that faded, a 16-block table, a 100-point scorecard and a planning template [p. 1-12].

Core claims [p. 1]: winners share six things (a verb understood in seconds, feedback on every input, a familiar base with one twist, a session that fits the device, cheap failure, a reason to come back). The last two changed most. "Phone and browser hits and PC hits now follow almost opposite recipes, so choose the platform before the genre."

Evidence labels used by the document [p. 1]: [counted] = from the tag table, each game weighted by how many top lists it appeared on; [research] = outside source listed in Sources; [judgement] = the author's reading.

## Frameworks, lists and checklists

### The five recipes [p. 1-2]

"Five kinds of game account for most of the 2020-2026 hits" [counted, judgement] [p. 1].

| Recipe | Core | Session | Failure and return | Look and sound | How it spreads | Ladder proof | Cost for you |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. One-board puzzle (daily or endless) | One grid, one rule: place, guess, merge or clear | 1-5 min | Short game-over with a best score, or one puzzle a day with a streak | Flat shapes, bright palette, crisp sound effects | Share grid, score, word of mouth | Tetris, 2048, Wordle, Block Blast, Suika, Connections | Tier 1-2. Cheapest; the hard part is a generator that stays fair |
| 2. Level puzzle (sort, pour, unscrew, fling) | Tap or drag to solve a small hand-made setup; physics or sorting does the satisfying part | 1-5 min a level | Retry the level, no penalty; a map, decorations or collection to return for | Bright, chunky, tactile; satisfying clicks and pops | Store charts, portals, ads on mobile | Angry Birds, Cut the Rope, Royal Match, Magic Sort, screw puzzles, Parking Jam | Tier 2-4. The levels are the work |
| 3. One-touch score chaser | One input (tap, hold or swipe) against obstacles that speed up | Under 1 min | Instant restart, best score, unlockable skins or a level goal | One strong silhouette, bright and readable at speed; punchy sound or music sync | Clips, streamers, beating friends' scores | Flappy Bird, Subway Surfers, Crossy Road, Geometry Dash, Chrome Dino | Tier 1-3. Feel tuning is the work |
| 4. Run builder (roguelite) | Simple action or card play; every few minutes pick 1 of 3 upgrades that combine | One 10-30 min run | The run ends, unlocks carry over | Pixel or hand-drawn, readable chaos; a strong soundtrack | Streamers and demos | Vampire Survivors, Balatro, Slay the Spire, Brotato, Hades, Nubby's Number Factory | Tier 4-6. | 5. Mostly interface and numbers; the curve takes tuning |

### Six ingredients every recipe needs [p. 2-3]

1. A verb you get in 5 seconds and play within 10. Web players leave if the first seconds are dull [research].
2. Feedback on every input: sound, motion and a number moving.
3. A familiar base plus one twist. Balatro is poker, Nubby's Number Factory is plinko, Raccoin is a coin pusher, Ball x Pit is Breakout.
4. A session that fits the device: under 5 minutes on phones and the web, 10-30 minute runs on PC.
5. Cheap failure: retry in a second, fail softly, or end the run but keep progress.
6. A reason to return: unlocks, a collection, a streak or tomorrow's puzzle.

### Ten practices of the modern hits [p. 5-6]

1. A simple core inside a meta layer. Upgrades, collections, decorating, streaks (Royal Match's decorating, Bridge Race's skins, Vampire Survivors' unlocks). Mobile studios call this "hybrid-casual" [research].
2. Cheap failure. Examples: Dorfromantik, Stardew Valley, Infinite Craft.
3. Pick one of three. Builds grow from small, frequent choices: Vampire Survivors, Balatro, Brotato, ROUNDS.
4. Remove a verb. Vampire Survivors dropped aiming and shooting; weapons fire on their own, which made it easy to pick up and playable for more people [research]. Loop Hero and Mob Control do the same.
5. A familiar toy plus a roguelite layer. Poker (Balatro), plinko (Nubby's Number Factory), coin pushers (Raccoin), Breakout (Ball x Pit), scratch cards (Scritchy Scratchy). Balatro's creator built on a card game he knew and avoided other deckbuilders so he would not copy them [research].
6. One finger on phones.
7. Once a day, and shareable. Wordle: one puzzle a day; the emoji grid was added after a player typed one by hand [research].
8. Playable in seconds. Poki: web players behave more like short-video viewers than Steam buyers. Aim for a small download and play within seconds [research].
9. Demos, festivals and streamers. Developers build wishlists with festivals, demos and streamer copies [research].
10. Short, cheap and idle. Nodebuster, Scritchy Scratchy, Rusty's Retirement.

### What didn't last [p. 6-7]

Common thread: "a format that relied on novelty or on one channel faded once the novelty or the channel went" [judgement] [p. 6].

| Trend | Peak | What happened | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypercasual (one-tap 3D games paid for by ads) | 2017-2021 | Share of casual mobile installs fell from about 50% (early 2021) to just over 30% (early 2023) [research]; 18 ladder hits cluster in 2018-2021, few after 2022 [counted] | A simple core alone no longer holds players; add a light meta layer |
| Survivor-like copies | 2022-2023 | 17 Steam hits in 2023, then gone from the top-10 genre lists in 2024 and 2025 [research] | Arriving late to a hit genre rarely works; remixing a familiar toy kept working (Balatro, Nubby's Number Factory, Raccoin) |
| Daily-puzzle clones | 2022 | Seven daily games charted in 2021-2024 [counted]; Wordle outlasted most copies [judgement] | A daily format needs its own twist and content you own. Heardle was built on licensed music |
| .io multiplayer arenas | 2015-2018 | Agar.io, Slither.io, Paper.io topped charts, then moved to offline versions against bots | Online-only needs a crowd you can't guarantee; bot versions survive |
| Platformers | 1985-1995 | 23% of 1990s weight, 6% in 2020-2026 [counted]; on Steam even the top 1% earn less than other genres [research] | Use one as a twist (a level that tricks you, physics, rhythm), not as the whole pitch |
| Arcade shooters and maze chases | 1978-1984 | 68% of 1970s weight, 12% in 2020-2026 [counted] | The thrill survived inside new structures: survivor-likes, one-touch chasers |
| Flash portals | 2003-2012 | Flash ended; browser play moved to HTML5 portals such as Poki, where the first seconds decide [research] | The browser still works, but load fast and start playing instantly |

Gems that stayed niche [p. 7]: in the Hidden Gems Pool, 109 of 116 proven ideas had only small fan clones [counted]. Usual reasons [judgement]: copies kept the original names and art, kept PC-only controls, added no twist and no reason to return, and had no channel to be found through.

### Block by block: the 16 blocks [p. 8-10]

"Winners kept the top layers simple and spent their effort on feedback, content and the reason to return" [p. 8].

| Block | What the winners did | Common mistake | Ladder examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Core loop | One verb with a clear goal and a reward within seconds. Depth comes from combinations, not more verbs | Several half-finished verbs | Vampire Survivors (move only), Flappy Bird (tap), Balatro (play a hand) |
| 2. Controls | On phones, one finger: 70% of 2020-2026 phone and browser hits use only tap or drag [counted]. On PC, mouse or keys | A virtual joystick plus buttons on a phone | Block Blast, Subway Surfers, Suika Game |
| 3. Feedback and feel | Every input answered with sound, motion and a number; big moments celebrated | Silent hits and still screens | Peggle's final shot, Vampire Survivors' chest opening, Balatro's score count-up |
| 4. Rules and systems | Few rules that interact; order or position matters | Rules that never touch each other | Balatro's jokers, Baba Is You, Suika merges |
| 5. Content | Many small pieces (weapons, jokers, levels) rather than a few big ones | One long hand-built level or art-heavy content | Balatro's 150 jokers [research], Cut the Rope's level boxes |
| 6. Difficulty | Quick start, readable ramp, losses that feel fair | A spike in the first minute | Wordle's six guesses, Geometry Dash's practice mode |
| 7. Progression | A meta layer in 50% of 2020-2026 hits [counted]: unlocks, collections, decorating, streaks | Progression that makes the core trivial or replaces it | Royal Match decorating, Vampire Survivors unlocks, Stardew Valley |
| 8. Sessions | Matched to the device: 83% of phone and browser hits run under 5 minutes; PC runs last 10-60 [counted] | A 20-minute minimum on a phone | Wordle's "three minutes a day" [research], Brotato's 20-minute runs |
| 9. Readability and UI | State readable at a glance: big shapes, strong contrast, numbers where they matter | Small text and clutter during fast play | Vita Mahjong's large tiles, Into the Breach's telegraphed attacks |
| 10. Art | Bright palettes in 69% of 2020-2026 hits; phone hits chunky and flat, PC hits pixel or hand-drawn (56%) [counted] | Mixed styles from mismatched assets | Block Blast (flat), Balatro (pixel), Hades (hand-drawn) |
| 11. Audio | Phone hits lean on punchy sound effects (70%); PC hits on soundtrack and ambience (80%) [counted] | No sound, or one loop that grates | Peggle's Ode to Joy, Geometry Dash's music sync, Stardew Valley |
| 12. Theme | Abstract (25%) and comedy or meme themes (14%) lead the 2020s; animals and food are easy, warm hooks [counted] | A theme that hides the rules | Suika Game (fruit), Brotato (a potato), Cult of the Lamb (cute and dark) |
| 13. Onboarding | Teach by playing; web players decide in seconds [research] | A text tutorial before any play | Vampire Survivors needs no tutorial [research] |
| 14. Menus | One tap to play; restart in under a second | Menus between retries | Super Meat Boy's instant retry, Chrome Dino |
| 15. Saving | Best score, unlocks and streaks kept between visits | Progress lost on refresh | Wordle's streak, Cookie Clicker's save |
| 16. Platform | Loads fast, playable in seconds, works in portrait on phones [research] | Asking players to rotate their phone or wait for a big download | Poki's own guidance for web games |

### Score your idea: the 10 checks [p. 11-12]

Each check 0-10 points. "80 or more: build it. 65-79: fix the lowest checks first. Under 65: rethink the core before writing code" [judgement] [p. 11].

| # | Check | Full marks when | Zero when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verb in 5 seconds | A stranger gets the action from one screenshot; one finger or one key on phones | It needs a paragraph of explanation |
| 2 | Fun within 10 seconds | Play starts on the first tap; no menu wall | Logos, menus and a tutorial come first |
| 3 | Feedback on every input | Every action gets sound, motion and a visible change | Inputs pass silently |
| 4 | Familiar base plus one twist | People recognise the base and are surprised by one change | Either a straight copy or nothing familiar at all |
| 5 | Session fits the device | Under 5 minutes on phones and the web; 10-30 minute runs on PC | Long minimum sessions on a phone |
| 6 | Cheap failure | Retry within a second, soft failure, or a run that ends but keeps progress | Losing wipes long progress or needs a reload |
| 7 | Choices that matter | Every minute holds a real decision: what to build, where to place, when to risk | Play is only reflexes or only waiting |
| 8 | Reason to return | Unlocks, a collection, a streak or tomorrow's puzzle | Nothing changes between visits |
| 9 | Readable look you can make | Bright, consistent style drawn in code; state readable at a glance | Needs art you can't produce, or the screen is cluttered |
| 10 | Built to spread | A shareable result, a clip-worthy moment or chaos streamers enjoy | Nothing worth showing anyone |

### Eras, method and sources [p. 3-4, 12-14]

- Eras: arcade games led the first two decades, platformers the late 1980s and 1990s, puzzles took over once games moved into browsers and onto phones in the 2000s; the 2020s added roguelites as the new PC leader [p. 3]. "The platform split matters more than any genre trend" [p. 4].
- Tagging: all 485 games got genre, sub-genre, theme, view, input, session length and how they spread; the 199 games from 2020-2026 or on 3 or more top lists also got art, palette, loop structure, failure style, progression and sound [p. 12].
- Weighting: a game counts once per top list it appeared on (Subway Surfers, on 9 lists, counts nine times) [p. 13].
- Owner's earlier work used: Remake Ladder 1970-2026 (485 games), Hidden Gems Pool (116 ideas and their gap ratings), Game Building Blocks (16 blocks) [p. 13].
- Outside sources named: How To Market A Game (Steam hits by genre 2022-2025; platformer sales 2022), GameDiscoverCo (Steam tags 2021 vs 2025), Game World Observer (survivor-likes in decline, first half of 2024), GamesBeat / Liftoff report (hypercasual install share 50% to 30%), PocketGamer.biz (hypercasual retention and level-count bar in 2025), Game Developer, TechCrunch and Boston.com (Wordle), GamesRadar ("Diablo creator on Vampire Survivors: simple controls, no tutorial needed"), PC Gamer (Vampire Survivors creator interview: "inspired by Magic Survival"), GamesRadar and saschb2b (Balatro), Poki ("What it takes to build web games in 2026: the first seconds decide"), Defold ("Best practices for Poki: under 20 MB, gameplay within seconds") [p. 13-14].

## What it means for Vampire: No Survivors

### (a) Process for an AI building alone

- Fill the planning template, then score the idea on the 10 checks before coding; act on the thresholds and treat the score as a weak-point finder only [p. 11-12].
- The template's last line is "First version = core loop only: [what is in it, what waits]" [p. 12]. This matches the owner's first stop; write the "what waits" list explicitly (inference).
- Spend effort where winners did: feedback, content, the reason to return; keep the top layers simple [p. 8].
- Recipe 4 is the most expensive recipe (Tier 4-6): "light on art, heavy on systems and balance" [p. 2]. Budget most time for systems and balance (inference).
- Check 9 rewards a "bright, consistent style drawn in code" [p. 12]: code-drawn shapes are a legitimate final look, not only a prototype stage (inference).

### (b) Core loop, controls and feel

- One verb (Vampire Survivors: move only), clear goal, reward within seconds; depth from combinations, not more verbs [p. 8]. "Remove a verb": attacks fire on their own [p. 5].
- One finger, tap or drag; the named mistake is "a virtual joystick plus buttons on a phone" [p. 8]. A drag-to-move thumb control with no extra buttons fits (inference).
- Every input gets sound, motion and a number; celebrate big moments (the chest opening is the cited Vampire Survivors example) [p. 8]. "Silent hits and still screens" is the mistake [p. 8].
- Play starts on the first tap, no menu wall, no text tutorial; restart in under a second with no menus between retries [p. 9-11].
- Every minute must hold a real decision ("what to build, where to place, when to risk"); play that is "only reflexes or only waiting" scores zero [p. 11]. Recipe 4 says pick 1 of 3 "every few minutes" [p. 2], practice 3 says "small, frequent choices" [p. 5]. For short phone runs the pick should come at least once a minute (inference).

### (c) Content, difficulty, builds and balance

- Upgrades must combine [p. 2]; few rules that interact, order or position matters; "rules that never touch each other" is the mistake [p. 8].
- Many small pieces (weapons) rather than a few big ones [p. 8].
- Quick start, readable ramp, losses that feel fair; no spike in the first minute [p. 8].
- The run ends but unlocks carry over [p. 2]. Progression must not make the core trivial or replace it [p. 9].
- The three checks that separate lasting from faded games are twist, real choices and reason to return [p. 10]. A straight copy scores zero on check 4 [p. 11].
- A theme must not hide the rules; "cute and dark" (Cult of the Lamb) is a cited working theme [p. 9].

### (d) Phone: layout, input, performance, audio

- Portrait; never ask to rotate; fast load, no big download [p. 10]; under 20 MB per the Poki source line [p. 14].
- Session tension: phone norm is under 5 minutes (83%) and a "20-minute minimum on a phone" is a mistake [p. 4, 9], yet the document allows recipe 4 in a browser at 10-15 minute runs [p. 3]. A 10-15 minute run does not earn full marks on check 5 [p. 11].
- State readable at a glance: big shapes, strong contrast, numbers where they matter; no small text or clutter during fast play [p. 9]. Recipe 4 wants "readable chaos" [p. 2].
- Phone hits are bright (84%), chunky and flat, and effects-led in sound (70%) [p. 4, 9]. Recipe 4's "pixel or hand-drawn" look and "strong soundtrack" are PC norms [p. 2]; on a phone, favour punchy effects over music (inference). A dark vampire palette works against the bright-palette norm (inference).
- Avoid "no sound, or one loop that grates" [p. 9].
- Save best score and unlocks; never lose progress on refresh [p. 10].

### (e) Testing and playtests

- The only tests given are the 10-check score and its retro-test on ten games [p. 10-12]. Check 1 is testable: "a stranger gets the action from one screenshot" [p. 11].
- Vampire Survivors itself scored 94, weakest on Look (7) [p. 10].
- Limits: tags are judgement; palette and sound least certain; 118-game sample, so "a difference of a few percentage points means little"; the scoring test was built from the hits it scores [p. 13].

### (f) Market, positioning, name, release

- "Survivor-like copies" is listed as a faded trend: "Arriving late to a hit genre rarely works" [p. 6]. The genre's thrill itself survives [p. 7]. The game needs one clear twist.
- Recipe 4 spreads by streamers and demos [p. 2]; phone hits spread mainly by ads (68%) and almost never by streamers or press (2%) [p. 4]. A phone web survivor-like has no proven free channel in this data (inference).
- Clones stayed niche when they "kept the original names and art" and added no twist [p. 7]. The working title sits close to Vampire Survivors; the owner has kept it for now (owner instruction, not this document), so this is a point to revisit before any release (inference).
- Check 10: plan "a clip-worthy moment or chaos streamers enjoy" [p. 12].

## Warnings and anti-patterns

- Choosing genre before platform [p. 1].
- A simple core with no meta layer; novelty or a single channel as the whole plan [p. 6].
- Late straight copies of a hit genre; copies keeping original names, art and PC-only controls [p. 6-7].
- Several half-finished verbs; joystick plus buttons; silent hits; rules that never interact; one long hand-built level; a first-minute difficulty spike; progression that trivialises the core; a 20-minute minimum on a phone; small text and clutter; mixed art styles; no sound or a grating loop; a theme that hides the rules; a text tutorial first; menus between retries; progress lost on refresh; asking to rotate the phone or wait for a big download [p. 8-10].
- Online-only play that needs a crowd; content you do not own (licensed music) [p. 7, 11].
- Reading the score as a forecast [p. 11].

## Things the lead should decide or check

1. Run length: under 5 minutes (phone norm) or 10-15 minutes (the document's browser allowance for recipe 4) [p. 3, 4]. A candidate axis for the three prototype variations (inference).
2. State the one twist in one sentence and score check 4 honestly; the document's warning about survivor-like copies applies directly [p. 6, 11].
3. Fill the planning template and record the score and lowest checks in the design document [p. 12].
4. Confirm one-thumb drag with no extra buttons [p. 8].
5. Name "the choice each minute" and measure how often a pick-1-of-3 actually arrives [p. 11, 12].
6. Measure time from page open to first input and to first reward against the 5 and 10 second marks; measure restart time against one second [p. 2, 10].
7. Palette: decide how a vampire theme stays bright, high-contrast and readable; pick 4-6 colours [p. 9, 12].
8. Sound: effects-led first [p. 9, 12].
9. Decide what the reason to return will be, even if it waits until after the core-loop prototype [p. 3, 12].
10. Save on every change that matters, so a refresh loses nothing [p. 10].
11. Tier meanings and build-time figures are in the Remake Ladder, not here; check the other owner documents [p. 2, 12].
