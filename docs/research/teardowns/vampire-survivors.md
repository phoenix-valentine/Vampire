# Teardown: Vampire Survivors (poncle), including the mobile version

**Research status, 2026-11-26.** Web search worked; page fetching did not (vampire.survivors.wiki,
en.wikipedia.org, apps.apple.com and play.google.com are outside this session's network allowlist),
and the shared search budget ran out after about 35 queries. Every sourced fact below therefore comes
from a search-result summary of the cited page, not from a full read. `[n]` points to the source
list. **(M)** means "from memory, unverified". "Not found" means searched for and not found.
Quotations are as the search summaries gave them and may not be word-exact. The game was not played
in this session.

## Worksheet

- **Game:** Vampire Survivors. **Genre:** bullet heaven (auto-shooter with roguelite unlocks),
  inspired by the Android game Magic Survival [9].
- **Platforms and price:** Steam, USD 4.99 (M); Xbox, Switch, PlayStation (M); iOS and Android, free,
  with optional rewarded ads only (a revive, extra gold after a run) and add-ons of about USD 2 [3][10].
- **Release:** Steam early access 2021-12-17; version 1.0 2022-10-20 [11]; mobile 2022-12-08 [10].
- **Review standing:** Steam "Overwhelmingly Positive", 98% of 263,481 (Steambase, 2026-06-28) [1].
  Google Play about 4.4 to 4.5 from 76,000 to 79,000 ratings, 5 to 8 million installs (three
  trackers, 2025 to 2026) [2]. App Store 4.6 in the US (AppFollow, undated; count not found) [3].
  Metacritic PC 87, Xbox Series 95, iOS 91 (via Wikipedia, undated) [12]. TouchArcade 4.5 of 5
  (2022-12-09) [13]. BAFTA Best Game and Game Design, 2023 [14].
- **Core sentence:** "You move, and only move, to stay alive for 30 minutes while a horde that
  thickens every minute closes in from all sides."

**What players love**

1. The arc from one weak weapon to a "self-perpetuating power fantasy" in the last third [15]. Block 6.
2. Discovering weapon and passive pairings, and the drip of unlocks (M). Block 7.
3. One stick and nothing else: "Single stick masterpiece" (Game Informer); portrait thumb play (TouchArcade) [13][15]. Block 2.
4. The treasure chest as a staged jackpot, built on the creator's slot-machine work [9]. Block 3.
5. "One more run" at an honest price: it "doesn't have a hand perpetually on your wallet" (App Store review) [3][15]. Blocks 8, 16.

**What players complain about**

1. Runs drag once the build is overpowered (PC Gamer: that is when it "dragged") [15]. Block 6.
2. "A slow, rough opening and difficulty curve" (a TV Tropes user review; Hardcore Gamer praises the same thing as "a long slow burn") [15][16]. Block 6.
3. Late-run clutter: effects bury the player and the enemies (player reviews, exact page not confirmed; also (M)) [16]. Block 9.
4. On phones, aimed weapons point where the thumb steers; touch "not the most responsive"; lag with many sprites (App Store review, Pocket Gamer) [3][13]. Blocks 2, 16.
5. On phones, the run is lost if the system closes the app (148Apps, App Store reviews) [3][13]. Block 15.

## The first minute

Reconstructed from the numbers above and memory; not timed.

- **Before 0:00.** Title, start, the one character, the one stage: a few presses, nothing to read (M).
- **0:00.** The character stands at the centre of an open field. No prompt. The whip cracks sideways by itself every 1.35 s.
- **0:02 to 0:05.** Bats walk in from the edges; the game keeps at least 15 alive. The player moves; a bat meets the whip, dies in one hit, drops a blue gem.
- **0:05 to 0:15.** The player walks over gems and the top bar fills: the reward must be fetched.
- **About 0:10 to 0:20 (M).** First level-up: pause, three cards. Next levels cost 15 and 25 XP, so two or three more arrive before 1:00 (M).
- **0:20 to 1:00.** Circling; a brazier breaks into a coin or food (M). Almost no danger.
- **1:00.** Zombies arrive, the floor rises to 30 alive, a boss bat appears [5]; its chest is the first jackpot (M).

## For our game

**Three ideas to take**

1. **Reward at the kill site.** Blood left at the body makes every kill a choice between drinking now and the hunters closing in: our core sentence as one mechanic.
2. **An authored night with a hard end.** One new pressure per row, named events, dawn in the Reaper's role, explained by the fiction. Compress 30 rows to 8 or 10.
3. **A staged jackpot and an open pair rule.** One rare, theatrical feed; powers that complete each other, with the pairing shown on the card.

**Three mistakes to avoid**

1. **The plateau.** The last third plays itself. Keep the verb alive (fleeing prey, witnesses) and let hunger make standing still cost blood.
2. **Effects over information.** Nothing draws above the player or a hunter; cap effect opacity and count.
3. **A phone-hostile shell.** Long runs, a menu chain, a run lost when the app closes, facing-aim, and five opening minutes without threat.

**What it would take to beat it**

- **Builds and choices.** Not its strongest area. Visible pairings, fewer than 1 in 5 obvious level-ups, and one offer per level-up that changes how the player moves.
- **Pacing and fairness.** It is the bar. Deliver its arc (weak, threatened, dominant, ended) in a third of the time: first danger by 0:30, first spike by 2:00, no 20 seconds where standing still is best, every death caused by something visible 2 seconds earlier, and threat that rises with the player's success (witnesses), not only the clock.
- **Readability on a phone.** Its late run would likely fail our readability reader; pass 5 of 5 at the busiest moment. Its 300 cap suits a wide screen; ours must be far lower at 390 pixels (my proposal, to be measured).
- **Instant one-thumb play.** It is one-thumb but not instant. One tap from load to moving, a kill inside 5 seconds, a choice inside 20, cards in the thumb's half, no facing-aim, a run that survives a closed tab.
- **Theme that explains the rules.** It does not try. One fiction sentence per rule, and 4 of 5 on the theme reader.
