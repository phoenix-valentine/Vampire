# Teardown: Vampire Survivors (poncle), including the mobile version

**Research status, 2026-11-26.** Web search worked. Page fetching did not: every host tried
(vampire.survivors.wiki, en.wikipedia.org, apps.apple.com, play.google.com) is outside this session's
network allowlist, and the shared search budget ran out after about 35 queries. So every sourced fact
below comes from a search-result summary of the cited page, not from reading the page in full.
`[n]` points to the source list. **(M)** means "from memory, unverified". "Not found" means searched
for and not found. I did not play the game in this session.

## Worksheet

- **Game:** Vampire Survivors. **Genre:** bullet heaven (survivor-like auto-shooter with roguelite
  unlocks), inspired by the Android game Magic Survival [9].
- **Platforms and price:** PC on Steam, USD 4.99 (M); Xbox, Switch, PlayStation (M); iOS and Android,
  free, with optional rewarded ads only (a revive, extra gold after a run) and paid add-ons of about
  USD 1.79 to 2.49 [3][10].
- **Release:** browser version on itch.io 2021 (M); Steam early access 2021-12-17; version 1.0
  2022-10-20 [12]; mobile 2022-12-08, unannounced [10].
- **Review standing:** Steam "Overwhelmingly Positive", 98% of 263,481 reviews (Steambase tracker,
  updated 2026-06-28) [1]. Google Play about 4.4 to 4.5 from 76,000 to 79,000 ratings, 5 to 8 million
  installs (three trackers, 2025 to 2026; store page not read) [2]. App Store 4.6 in the US
  (AppFollow, undated; rating count not found) [3]. Metacritic PC 87, Xbox Series 95, iOS 91 (via
  Wikipedia, undated) [13]. TouchArcade 4.5 of 5 (2022-12-09) [14]. BAFTA Best Game and Game Design,
  2023 [15].
- **Core sentence:** "You move, and only move, to stay alive for 30 minutes while a horde that
  thickens every minute closes in from all sides."

**What players love**

1. The arc from one weak weapon to a "self-perpetuating power fantasy" in the last third [16]. Block 6.
2. Build discovery: pairing a weapon with a passive item to transform it, and the drip of unlocks (M). Block 7.
3. One stick, nothing else: "Single stick masterpiece" (Game Informer); portrait play "with your thumb" (TouchArcade) [14][16]. Block 2.
4. The treasure chest as a staged jackpot, built from the creator's slot-machine experience [9]. Block 3.
5. "One more run" that is also relaxing [16], at an honest price: a mobile reviewer says it "doesn't have a hand perpetually on your wallet" [3]. Blocks 8 and 16.

**What players complain about**

1. Runs drag once the build is overpowered; an easy start that becomes a steamroll is when it "dragged" (PC Gamer) [16]. Block 6.
2. A slow, weak opening: "a long slow burn" (Hardcore Gamer) [16]. Block 6.
3. Late-run visual clarity: weapon effects bury the player and the enemies (player reviews found by search, exact page not confirmed; also (M)) [17]. Block 9.
4. On phones, directional weapons aim where the thumb steers; touch is "not the most responsive" and many sprites cause lag (Pocket Gamer) [3][14]. Blocks 2 and 16.
5. On phones, a run is lost if the system closes the app (148Apps, App Store reviewers) [14][3]. Block 15.

## The first minute

Reconstructed from the wiki numbers above and memory. Not timed by me.

- **Before 0:00.** Title, start, the one unlocked character, the one stage. A few presses, no text to read (M).
- **0:00.** The character stands at the centre of an open field. No prompt. The whip cracks sideways on its own every 1.35 s.
- **0:02 to 0:05.** Bats walk in from the screen edges, one new bat per second, up to 15 alive. The player moves, a bat touches the whip arc and dies in one hit, a blue gem drops.
- **0:05 to 0:15.** The player walks over gems and sees the top bar fill. Lesson learned without words: the reward must be fetched.
- **About 0:10 to 0:20 (M).** First level-up. The game pauses and shows three cards. Next levels cost 15 and 25 XP, so two or three more follow before 1:00 (M).
- **0:20 to 1:00.** Kiting in small circles; a brazier breaks into a coin or food (M). Almost no danger.
- **1:00.** Zombies arrive, the floor rises to 30 alive, and a boss bat appears [5]; its chest is the first jackpot (M).

## For our game

**Three ideas to take**

1. **The reward lies where the kill happened.** Blood left at the body makes every kill a choice between drinking now and the hunters closing in. That is our core sentence in one mechanic.
2. **An authored, minute-marked night with a hard end.** One new pressure per row, named events, and dawn in the Reaper's role, which the fiction explains for free. Compress 30 rows into 8 to 10.
3. **One staged jackpot and an open pair rule.** A rare, theatrical feed, and powers that complete each other with the pairing printed on the card.

**Three mistakes to avoid**

1. **The plateau.** The last third plays itself. Keep the verb alive (prey that flees, witnesses to stop) and let hunger make standing still cost blood.
2. **Effects over information.** Never draw an effect above the player or a hunter; cap effect opacity and count.
3. **A phone-hostile shell.** 30-minute runs, a menu chain, a run lost when the app closes, facing-aimed attacks, and a first five minutes with no threat.

**What it would take to beat it, by scorecard area**

- **Builds and choices.** Not this game's strongest area: offers become obvious once pairings are known. Beat it with visible pairings, fewer than 1 in 5 obvious level-ups, and at least one offer per level-up that changes how the player moves.
- **Pacing and fairness.** It is the bar. Deliver the same arc (weak, threatened, dominant, ended) in a third of the time: first danger by 0:30, first spike by 2:00, no 20 seconds where standing still is best, every death caused by something visible in the 2 seconds before it, and threat that rises with the player's own success (witnesses), not only the clock.
- **Readability on a phone.** Its late run would likely fail our readability reader. Beat it by passing 5 of 5 at the busiest moment. The 300 cap was set for a wide screen; ours must be far lower on 390 pixels, with colour reserved for danger (my proposal, to be measured).
- **Instant one-thumb play.** It has one thumb but not instant. Beat it with one tap from load to moving, a kill inside 5 seconds, a choice inside 20, cards in the thumb's half of the screen, no facing-aim, and a run that survives a closed tab.
- **Theme that explains the rules.** It does not try. Beat it by giving every rule one fiction sentence and passing the theme reader 4 of 5.

**Flag for the lead.** Search results showed an existing itch.io page at `reaktori.itch.io/vampire-no-survivors`. Not opened. Check before the public name is settled.
