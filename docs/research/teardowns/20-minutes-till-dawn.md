# Teardown: 20 Minutes Till Dawn

Research note, 2026-11-26. Web search worked (35 queries). Opening pages in full did not (the session's network refuses the store, wiki and press hosts), and the shared search budget ran out before the queries on phone orientation, enemy roster, audio and character list. Every sourced fact and quote below comes from a search-result summary of the cited page, so quotes may be paraphrased. "(memory)" means from memory, unverified. "Not found" means searched without result, or not reached.

## Worksheet

- Game: 20 Minutes Till Dawn (free prologue: 10 Minutes Till Dawn). Developer flanne, solo; built and marketed in about two months after five years on a tactics game [S3][S5].
- Genre: survivor-like roguelite, played as a twin-stick shooter.
- Platforms and price: Windows, macOS (Steam, Epic) 4.99 USD [S2]; 2.99 USD in early access [S9]. iOS and Android: free with reward ads and purchases, plus a separate Premium app at 4.99 USD (publisher Erabit Studios) [S8]. Switch 4.99 USD [S10].
- Release: Steam early access 8 June 2022, version 1.0 on 8 June 2023 [S3]. Phones September 2022 (9th in Wikipedia, 15th in Pocket Gamer) [S3][S9]. Switch 21 December 2023 [S10].
- Review standing (all read 2026-11-26; the snapshots' own dates are unknown):
  - Steam: Very Positive, 91% of 26,390; last 30 days 78% of 133 [S1].
  - Google Play: free app 4.6 from 147K to 158K reviews; Premium 4.8 from 21.9K [S7].
  - App Store: free app 4.8 from about 8.6K ratings; Premium about 4.4 from about 1,500 (aggregators) [S8].
  - Critics: OpenCritic 77, 50% recommend; Softpedia 7.5/10 [S23].

Core sentence: "You aim, shoot and reload to survive until a 20-minute clock runs out, while a growing horde closes in and every shot slows you down."

### What players love

1. Manual aim and reload make it a skill game, not an auto-battler: "requires more constant vigilance" [S5][S21][S22]. Blocks 1, 2.
2. Upgrade trees and synergies that snowball into "broken combos"; "an admirable layer of depth" [S25][S24]. Block 7.
3. Bosses are real encounters with their own patterns [S21]. Blocks 5, 6.
4. The near-monochrome night look: "retro and moody" [S21][S27]. Block 10.
5. Low price, short sessions, runs well on Steam Deck [S26][S14]. Blocks 16, 8.

### What players complain about

1. Sameness: "after my first win ... every run is basically the same"; "core loop doesn't evolve" [S26][S23]. Block 5.
2. Too easy once permanent upgrades are bought: "almost impossible to lose" [S26]. Blocks 6, 7.
3. The dark, limited palette is hard to read once the screen is crowded [S27]. Block 9.
4. On phone: slowdown in hectic moments even on an iPhone 13 Pro; the 10-minute mode is still too long; no mid-run save [S24]. Blocks 16, 8, 15.
5. "Weapon choice doesn't feel important" [S23]; some dislike the slow walk while firing and the reload animation (machine summary, weak source) [S26]. Blocks 4, 2.

## The first minute (memory, unverified)

- Before play: title, loadout with one character and one gun available, map. About three taps.
- 0 to 3 s: a black field; the character stands in a small pool of light. Clock counting down from 20:00, hearts, 6 rounds.
- 3 to 10 s: a few slow creatures drift in from the edges. The player aims, fires, and notices that walking slows while firing. Two shots per kill.
- 10 to 15 s: the gun is empty. One second of reload, the first moment of exposure.
- 15 to 30 s: kills leave XP on the ground; collecting it means stepping toward where the enemies were.
- 30 to 45 s: first level-up. The game pauses on 5 cards.
- 45 to 60 s: density is still low. Nothing on screen can kill a careful player yet.

## For our game

Three ideas to take:

1. The attack costs mobility. Their gun slows the shooter; our feed should slow or hold the vampire while the blood comes in. It is the same fire-or-flee decision, made with one thumb and no aim stick, and it is our core sentence: drink to grow while hunters close in.
2. The title is the timer, with fixed beats. Dawn as a visible countdown, an elite and a boss at known times, and a closing safe area late. For us the shrinking ring is free: sunlight advances and the dark shrinks.
3. Small trees with the next tier in sight. Three-tier trees and a handful of named pairings let a player plan a build from the first card. On a phone: 3 cards, the next tier printed on the card.

Three mistakes to avoid:

1. Porting a two-hand skill to a phone and patching it with auto-aim. The skill either costs a second thumb or disappears.
2. Darkness as decoration that hides information, and a palette too narrow to separate threat from reward in a crowd.
3. Permanent power that erases the challenge, on top of thin run-to-run variety, with a run too long to finish on a phone.

What it would take to beat it:

- Builds and choices (its strongest area): every card must change what the thumb does within ten seconds; a reroll for every character; at least three distinct builds reachable in a 6-minute run; one evolution choice near the midpoint.
- Pacing and fairness: first kill inside 5 s, first level-up inside 30 s, a named beat every 60 to 90 s, every death traceable to a telegraph the player could see, and no permanent raw power.
- Readability on a phone: pass the cold-reader test on the busiest frame; one colour for danger; the lit area never hides threats behind the thumb.
- Instant one-thumb play: upright, one thumb, moving within 5 s of load, no selection screens before the first run.
- Theme that explains the rules: dawn must kill, not just end the clock; blood must be the one resource for growth and health; hunters must arrive because of what the player did.
